81.5 Million Americans Are Traveling for Thanksgiving — Starting Today

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 24, 2026

Thanksgiving week is here, and the roads and airports are about to fill up. AAA projects 81.5 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles from home during the Thanksgiving holiday period, which runs from today, Tuesday, November 24, through Monday, November 30. Thanksgiving itself falls on Thursday, November 26.

That number is a hair lower than last year — 81.6 million people traveled for Thanksgiving in 2025, so this year's forecast is down 0.1%, about 121,000 fewer travelers. It's the first dip since 2020, ending a three-year streak of record Thanksgiving travel that started in 2023. But don't mistake a small dip for a quiet week: 81.5 million is still a near-record number, and it's 3.7 million more travelers than before the pandemic.

Most of those people are driving. AAA projects 72.9 million Americans will travel by car — nearly 90% of all Thanksgiving travelers — down slightly from 73.1 million last year. AAA says this season's record high gas prices are part of the reason for the drop. Another 5.96 million will fly, down 0.2% from last year, accounting for more than 7% of all travelers. The one category still growing is everything else: 2.6 million Americans will travel by bus, train, or cruise, up 4.1% from last year, and AAA's new cruise forecast projects a record number of Americans taking cruises.

If you're hitting the road, timing matters. According to transportation data provider INRIX, the busiest days on the road will be today and Wednesday as most travelers head out, and Sunday and Monday when most people come home. If you can leave early in the morning or travel on Thanksgiving Day itself, you'll have a much easier drive.

For contractors and small business owners, this week is a strange one. A lot of jobsites shut down Wednesday through the weekend, which means a few days of forced rest whether you want it or not — and a chance to catch up on the paperwork that never gets done when the tools are running. If you've got crews on the road visiting family, the same advice applies to them as to everybody else: leave early, check the tires, and don't try to beat the traffic by driving tired.

One more thing worth noting for anybody traveling today: the Thanksgiving storm headed for the East Coast is bringing rain and strong winds from Wednesday through Saturday, and the FAA's air-traffic problems haven't gone away either. Check your flight status before you leave for the airport, and check the weather before you leave the driveway. The turkey will wait. Getting there safe is the part that matters.
