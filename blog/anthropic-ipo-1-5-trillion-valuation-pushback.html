Investors Are Balking at Anthropic's $1.5 Trillion IPO Price Tag

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 7, 2026

Anthropic, the AI company behind the Claude chatbot, is getting ready to go public — and the people who would have to buy the shares are pushing back on the price. Investors are telling Anthropic and its bankers that a valuation of roughly $1.5 trillion to $1.6 trillion is what they could support, down from the roughly $2 trillion figure that circulated through most of October. Some investors think even $1.5 trillion is a stretch, with one suggesting a figure closer to $1 trillion.

That is not a small argument. It is a negotiation over one of the largest listings ever attempted, and it is happening right as the stock market is questioning how much AI exposure it wants to hold.

What the filing actually shows

Anthropic filed its public Form S-1 on October 26 and an amended S-1/A on November 2 that updated its financials through the third quarter of 2026. The filing shows third-quarter revenue of about $17 billion, up 47 percent from $11.6 billion in the second quarter. But the third quarter also swung to a $567 million operating loss, after $974 million of operating income in the second quarter, driven by costlier short-term purchases of computing power.

The filing also revealed that Anthropic has committed approximately $895.4 billion toward AI computing power, chips, and other infrastructure needed to develop and run its models. That is the heart of the investors' concern: the company has to keep raising enormous sums to pay for the computing behind its models, and it needs the IPO to work to do it.

Anthropic's last official valuation was $965 billion, set in late May in a $65 billion funding round led by Altimeter Capital, Dragoneer, Greenoaks, and Sequoia. The IPO was at one point expected to seek an offering of around $100 billion. The Wall Street Journal reported that Anthropic could now seek around $80 billion instead.

The roadshow is the next test

The roadshow — the round of presentations to funds that comes before pricing — was expected to start the week of November 9, with pricing the following week, according to Reuters. But four people familiar with the investor discussions told Reuters that potential investors are raising doubts about whether Anthropic can proceed on that schedule in time for a November listing. Investors are also waiting on Anthropic's October revenue figure, which the company left blank in its updated S-1 filing.

Investor Michael Burry, known for betting against the U.S. housing market before the 2008 financial crisis, has expressed reservations about participating in the IPO. Anthropic has declined to comment on the investor doubts.

It is worth saying plainly what has not been decided. No final price range or share count has been set. No date is official. The company plans to list its Class A shares on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, and a debut is expected within a few weeks — but every figure in this story is an estimate from people who want to buy, not a price Anthropic has published.

For regular folks, the takeaway is simple. The AI boom is real money, real revenue, and real costs all at once. The same week the market sold off AI stocks, the biggest AI listing on the calendar hit a wall of buyers saying "not at that price." That is how markets are supposed to work — and it is a reminder that even the hottest company in the hottest industry has to convince buyers the numbers add up.
