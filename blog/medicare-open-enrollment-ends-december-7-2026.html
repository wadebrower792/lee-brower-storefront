Medicare Open Enrollment Ends December 7 — and This Year the Stakes Are Higher Than Usual

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 22, 2026

If you or someone you love is on Medicare, here's the date that matters: Monday, December 7, 2026. That's the last day of open enrollment. Whatever plan you pick — or whatever plan you fail to pick — takes effect January 1, 2027, for the full calendar year. Your plan has to receive your request by December 7. After that, the door closes.

Medicare covers roughly 70 million people — mostly folks 65 and older, plus some younger people with disabilities or certain health conditions. Open enrollment runs October 15 through December 7 every year. During that window you can join, drop, or switch a Medicare Advantage plan, switch between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage, or join, drop, or switch a Part D prescription drug plan.

This year, waiting until the last minute carries more risk than usual. A slew of Medicare Advantage plans are disappearing for next year. According to an analysis of federal data by KFF, the health policy research organization, 193 counties across the country will have no Medicare Advantage plans offered at all in 2027 — most of them rural — and an analysis by the An Arm and A Leg podcast found at least 2 million people will be affected by plans closing in their markets.

If your plan is ending and you don't choose another, the insurance company will assign you to another Medicare Advantage plan, or the government will enroll you in traditional Medicare — but without a supplemental or drug plan. One wrinkle this year: insurance companies can cap enrollment on some of the plans they do offer, so a plan you want could fill up if you wait.

Now the numbers for 2027. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services announced on November 17 that the standard Part B monthly premium will be $209.40 in 2027, up $6.50 from $202.90, and the annual Part B deductible will be $292, up $9 from $283. The yearly cap on what you pay out of pocket for covered Part D prescription drugs rises to $2,400, up from $2,100 — after that, you pay nothing more for covered drugs that year, though you still pay your plan premium. No Part D plan can have a deductible above $700 in 2027, up from $615. CMS projects the average Part D premium will rise less than $1 a month, but KFF found that enrollees who stay in their current prescription drug plan will see a 50 percent increase on average, from $34 to $51.

Here's a short checklist for the home stretch:

First, find your Annual Notice of Change. Your plan mailed it in the fall. It spells out what changes on January 1 — premiums, deductibles, copays, covered drugs, in-network doctors. Don't throw it out unread — consumer advocates say that's one of the costliest mistakes you can make. Can't find it? Call your insurer and ask for another copy.

Second, run your prescriptions through the Medicare Plan Finder at Medicare.gov/plan-compare. Enter every drug, the dose, and your pharmacy.

Third, call your doctors and ask one plain question: "Will you be in network with my plan in 2027?" Networks change every year.

Fourth, check the star rating. Plan Finder shows each plan's quality rating from 1 to 5 stars. If a 5-star plan is offered where you live, Medicare lets you switch into it once between December 8 and November 30 of the following year.

Fifth, get free help if you need it. Every state has a State Health Insurance Assistance Program — SHIP — with staff and volunteers who help seniors with Medicare for free. With so many people hunting for new plans this year, it may take longer than usual to get help, so don't wait.

And one warning that bears repeating every year: Medicare never calls to sell you anything, and never calls uninvited asking for personal information. Share your Medicare number only with people you trust who work with Medicare — your doctors, your insurer, or a licensed agent you've contacted yourself.

If you're happy with your plan and it's still offered, it renews automatically. But if your plan is ending or your drugs changed, fifteen days is not a lot of time — check it now, while there's still time to fix it.
