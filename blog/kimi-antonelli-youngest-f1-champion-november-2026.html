At 20 Years and 68 Days, Kimi Antonelli Is Formula 1's Youngest Champion Ever

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 5, 2026

Formula 1 has a new youngest world champion, and he did it by beating a record that stood for 16 years — by more than three full years.

On Sunday, November 1, Kimi Antonelli finished third at the Mexico City Grand Prix, and that was enough. The 20-year-old Mercedes driver clinched the 2026 drivers' championship with four races still to go in the season. He was 20 years and 68 days old — born August 25, 2006 — making him the youngest champion in the sport's 76-year history.

The old record belonged to Sebastian Vettel, who won his first title in 2010 at 23 years and 134 days old. Vettel himself sent Antonelli a video message after the race, congratulating the young Italian on breaking his mark.

How the title was won

Antonelli only needed to finish ninth or better in Mexico to lock up the championship. He did much better than that, coming home third behind race winner Max Verstappen of Red Bull and Lewis Hamilton of Ferrari. Verstappen won the Mexico City race for the sixth time; Hamilton started from pole position and took second.

When Antonelli crossed the line, his voice cracked over the team radio: "We did it, we did it." He was crying with joy as he took the checkered flag at the high-altitude Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez, his overall lead an unassailable 119 points over teammate George Russell, according to Reuters.

The race itself was wild — red-flagged and restarted, with two safety cars, multiple crashes, and a 33-minute halt with 25 of the 71 laps remaining. Antonelli was also battling illness and exhaustion in the days leading up to it, according to USA TODAY. None of it stopped him.

A record-breaking season

This was only Antonelli's second season in Formula 1. He replaced seven-time champion Lewis Hamilton at Mercedes in 2025, stepping into one of the biggest seats in the sport as a teenager. He answered by winning nine races this season — more than his teammate Russell's eight — including five wins in a row from China to Monaco, according to Reuters and The Drive.

He is now the 36th different driver to win the world championship, and only the fourth ever to do it in his second season, joining Lewis Hamilton, Jacques Villeneuve, and Juan Manuel Fangio.

He is also the first Italian to win the title since Alberto Ascari in 1953 — a 73-year wait. Antonelli is only the third Italian champion ever; the first was Giuseppe Farina, who won the very first championship in 1950. Italy celebrated on Monday, with hundreds of fans watching a screening of the race at the Checco Costa Museum at the Imola circuit.

What comes next

Antonelli isn't slowing down to enjoy it for long. He was already back at work this week, paying tribute to his childhood hero Ayrton Senna and spending time with Senna's family ahead of this weekend's São Paulo Grand Prix in Brazil. He has said his second-place finish at Interlagos in 2025 — then the best result of his career — laid the foundations for this championship year.

After Brazil, Formula 1 heads to Las Vegas, with two more races after that to close out the season. Antonelli has the title wrapped up, but the fight for the remaining wins — and for second in the standings — is still wide open.

A 20-year-old just became the youngest champion in the history of the fastest sport on earth. The record he broke may stand for a long time. The kid who broke it says he's only getting started.
