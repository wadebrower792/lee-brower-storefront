# California's King Tides Just Set Records. The Next Ones Could Be Higher.

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, October 31, 2026

This week the Pacific Ocean walked into California streets. Exceptionally high tides flooded coastal communities up and down the state starting October 26, 2026, closing sections of Pacific Coast Highway, pushing seawater into homes and businesses, and prompting Huntington Beach to declare a local emergency. The numbers from the tide gauges are what make this event different from an ordinary high tide: several stations set all-time records.

## What the gauges recorded

The National Weather Service recorded a water level of 2.60 meters — 8.52 feet above mean lower low water — at the San Diego tide gauge on Tuesday, October 27, the highest measurement in that station's record. It beat the previous day's reading of 2.57 meters (8.44 feet), which had itself been a record. In Huntington Beach, tides reached 2.50 meters (8.20 feet) during the week, flooding Huntington Harbor and Sunset Beach, two of the city's most exposed waterfront neighborhoods. An official city notice for October 28 announced closures on Pacific Coast Highway between Warner Avenue and Anderson Street and on Saybrook Lane between Edinger Avenue and Humboldt Drive.

The flooding was not confined to Southern California. The National Weather Service extended coastal flood advisories around San Francisco, San Pablo, and Monterey bays through November 1. On the Atlantic side, king tides and elevated seas flooded Florida's coast from October 26 to 29, with preliminary water levels reaching 2.7 feet above mean higher high water at Lake Worth Pier and 2.48 feet at South Port Everglades — both above the highest values in NOAA's current station tables for those gauges.

## Why it happened

A "king tide" is a non-scientific name people use for an exceptionally high tide, and these tides arrived with the full moon of late October. But the moon alone did not do this. The National Weather Service's San Diego office reported observed high tides running roughly 1.3 to 1.6 feet above astronomical predictions on October 29, meaning wind, swell, and elevated sea levels stacked on top of the already-high predicted tide. ABC News reported that king tides inundated parts of both U.S. coasts, flooding streets and sending seawater into homes and businesses in beach communities in California, Florida, and the Carolinas.

## What comes next matters for anyone who builds near water

The California Coastal Commission has designated winter king-tide observation periods for November 24–26 and December 23–25, 2026, and January 21–22, 2027. The National Weather Service's San Diego office noted that predicted astronomical high tides during those coming winter events would be higher than the ones associated with this week's flooding. That is the part worth sitting with: the October event set records, and the predicted tides for the winter windows are higher still.

For contractors and homeowners in low-lying coastal areas, the practical lessons are the same ones this week forced on Huntington Beach: know your elevation relative to the local tide gauge, not just the flood map; keep drainage and storm-drain paths clear before a predicted high-tide window, since seawater can come up through drains even without rain; and treat a predicted king tide plus onshore wind as a flooding event to prepare for, not a curiosity to watch. Water does not negotiate, and it does not care that it is not raining.
