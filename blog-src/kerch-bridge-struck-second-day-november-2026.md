# Ukraine Hit the Crimea Bridge Again — What We Know, and What We Don't

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 17, 2026

Ukraine struck the bridge linking Russia to Crimea again on Tuesday, the second day in a row, and this time the damage looks serious. But some of the most dramatic claims are still unconfirmed, and it's worth sorting out what's solid from what's not.

Here's what's confirmed. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy confirmed the attack himself on Tuesday, saying it was a response to Russian strikes on Ukrainian infrastructure, including bridges. He said the attack was the seventh stage of an operation that took weeks to plan, developed and coordinated by Ukraine's SBU security service and its General Staff. Ukrainian forces had spent weeks taking out air defenses, radars, and electronic warfare equipment along the way to clear a path. The final stage used Palianytsia drones against the road section and Neptune cruise missiles plus Ruta and FP-5 Flamingo drones against the rail section.

Sergei Aksyonov, the Russian-installed governor of Crimea, said two people were killed in the latest strike and that traffic across the bridge was suspended. The Kremlin said Monday's first attack in this round caused only minor damage. On Tuesday, Moscow did not say how serious the new damage was.

Now here's what we don't know yet. Videos and photos posted on social media — including some shared by Zelenskiy himself — appear to show the bridge's main road arch collapsed into the Kerch Strait below, which would block ships from passing underneath. Reuters, which reported the story, said it could not immediately verify that footage. So the collapse is plausible and widely shown, but not yet independently confirmed. Treat it as unconfirmed until a news organization or an official source verifies it on the ground.

Some background on why this bridge matters. The Crimea Bridge is 19 kilometers — about 12 miles — of road and rail linking Crimea to Russia's Krasnodar region. Russia built it after annexing Crimea in 2014, and it has been one of President Vladimir Putin's signature projects. It was once a key supply route for Russian forces in Ukraine. Ukraine has hit it before, but this week's operation is the most sustained effort against it yet.

The bridge attack came as part of what Russia's own Defense Ministry data suggests was the largest Ukrainian drone attack of the war. The ministry said 1,441 drones were shot down over 22 regions on Tuesday, beating Monday's figure of 1,336, which had itself set a record. At the same time, Russia pounded bridges and other infrastructure in Kyiv, the Ukrainian capital.

Two things to keep straight. First, the bridge is a military target in an active war — a supply route, not a civilian landmark — and both sides are treating it that way. Second, the fog of war is real: the first reports of a collapsed span may turn out right, or they may turn out to be less than they look. The honest move is to watch for verification, not to share the most dramatic video as if it's settled fact.
