# Veterans Day Is Wednesday, November 11: What's Closed, What's Open, and Why the Date Never Moves

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 2, 2026

Veterans Day 2026 falls on Wednesday, November 11. It is a federal holiday, and unlike Thanksgiving or Memorial Day, it never moves to a Monday or Friday. The date is the point: November 11 is the anniversary of the armistice that ended fighting in World War I, which took effect at the eleventh hour of the eleventh day of the eleventh month in 1918.

Because November 11 lands on a Wednesday this year, federal workers observe it on the day itself — no weekend shift needed. When November 11 falls on a Saturday, federal observance moves to the Friday before; when it falls on a Sunday, it moves to the Monday after. The holiday itself stays November 11 either way.

## What's closed and what's open

Federal offices close. Post offices close and there is no regular mail delivery. Federal Reserve Banks and the bond market close. Most banks close, and most public schools close — though state and local governments decide school closures for themselves, so check your local district.

The stock market stays open: the NYSE and Nasdaq trade on Veterans Day. Most stores and restaurants stay open, and many run veteran discounts and free meals — check the individual business before you go, because offers vary by location and usually require proof of service. Entry to national parks is free on Veterans Day, one of the fee-free days in the National Park Service calendar.

If you run a crew, plan for it now. A Wednesday holiday in the middle of the week splits the workweek, and deliveries, inspections, and permit offices that run on the federal calendar will be dark that day. Material suppliers and private businesses mostly keep normal hours, so the jobsite can keep moving — but anything that needs a federal office, a bank wire through the Fed, or a mail delivery waits until Thursday.

## How the holiday got here

The first Armistice Day was proclaimed by President Woodrow Wilson in November 1919, a year after the guns stopped. In 1938, Congress made November 11 a legal federal holiday. After World War II and Korea, the name changed: in 1954, Congress renamed it Veterans Day, so the day would honor veterans of all wars, not just World War I.

There was a detour. In 1968, Congress passed the Uniform Holiday Bill, which moved several holidays to Mondays for long weekends — and Veterans Day spent 1971 through 1977 observed on a Monday in late October. Veterans groups pushed back, and in 1975 President Gerald Ford signed Public Law 94-97, returning the observance to November 11 starting in 1978. It has stayed there ever since, whatever day of the week it falls on. The law is now in the books at 5 U.S.C. 6103, which lists Veterans Day, November 11, among the 11 federal holidays.

## Veterans Day is not Memorial Day

This is the mix-up that comes around every year, so plainly: Veterans Day honors everyone who has served in the U.S. Armed Forces — living or deceased, wartime or peacetime. Memorial Day, the last Monday in May, honors those who died in service. Armed Forces Day, in May, honors those currently serving. Three different days, three different purposes. Getting it right matters to the people being honored.

The national moment of silence is observed at 11:00 a.m. local time, with many observances marking 11:11 a.m. Communities across the country hold parades and ceremonies, and the national ceremony takes place at Arlington National Cemetery. If you know a veteran, the simplest observance is still the best one: say thank you, mean it, and if they want to talk about their service, listen.
