# Tesla Is Ending the Cybertruck — and Betting the Factory on Robotaxis

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 7, 2026

The Cybertruck is on its way out. On Friday, November 6, Tesla CEO Elon Musk posted on X that Tesla will wind down production of the Cybertruck at Gigafactory Texas around the middle of next year, so the plant can build more Cybercabs instead. The official Cybertruck account posted a video of the truck driving into the sunset the same morning, and Musk confirmed the truck would be discontinued next year.

That puts the end of the line at roughly mid-2027, about three and a half years after the first Cybertrucks were delivered. It is a short run for a vehicle Musk once promised would take on the pickups sold by Ford and General Motors.

## The sales never matched the plan

The numbers explain the decision better than any announcement does. Tesla built the Cybertruck line for 250,000 trucks a year. It has been selling at roughly a tenth of that. Cox Automotive counted just 11,586 Cybertruck sales in the United States through the first nine months of 2026, and Kelley Blue Book put last year's U.S. deliveries at 20,237, down 48 percent from 38,965 the year before.

Tesla does not break out Cybertruck deliveries separately. It lumps them into "Other Models," along with the Model S, Model X, and now the Semi. That group delivered 8,295 vehicles in the third quarter of 2026, down 48 percent from a year earlier and the lowest since the second quarter of 2021.

Earlier this year Tesla already stopped making the Model S and Model X at its Fremont, California, plant and pointed that capacity toward its Optimus humanoid robot. The Cybertruck move follows the same pattern: clear the floor for the next bet.

## What takes its place

The Cybercab is a gold-toned two-seater with no steering wheel and no pedals, built to drive itself. It is already on the road in Austin and Fort Worth as part of Tesla's robotaxi service, and Tesla aims to begin selling it to the public by the end of the year. The Texas plant has the manufacturing capacity to make 125,000 Cybertrucks a year, according to Musk, and that capacity can now be redirected to the Cybercab.

There are still open questions. Tesla has not said whether Cybertruck production will move to another plant or stop entirely, and Austin is the only known Tesla facility equipped to make the truck's specialized stainless-steel parts. Musk also said nothing about outstanding orders. What owners do have is a word from Cybertruck lead engineer Wes Morrill, who said Tesla will maintain spare parts and service support for trucks already on the road.

For a working man, the lesson is plain. The Cybertruck was a bold design and a big promise, and the market answered with its wallet. Tesla is not mourning it — it is clearing the floor for a robotaxi. Whether the Cybercab does what the Cybertruck could not is the next thing to watch, and it will be decided the same way: by how many people actually buy one.
