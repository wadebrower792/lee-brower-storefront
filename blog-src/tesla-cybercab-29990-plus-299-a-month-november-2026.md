# Tesla's Driverless Cybercab Is for Sale — $29,990, Plus $299 a Month, Forever

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 15, 2026

Tesla started inviting select customers to buy a Cybercab for personal use on Friday, November 13. It is the two-seat car with no steering wheel and no pedals — the one Tesla has been showing off as a robotaxi. Now you can own one. But the price tag on the screen is not the whole story, and the fine print matters more than the headline number.

Here is what Tesla's order page actually shows. The estimated purchase price is $29,990 before taxes. A $1,000 deposit is due at order, and it is non-refundable — change your mind and that money is gone. Delivery is estimated for December 2026 in Austin, Texas. And the car will not move without a $299-a-month Autonomy Subscription. The first month is included. After that, it is $299 every month until you cancel, which works out to $3,588 a year at the listed rate. Regular vehicle maintenance is also required to operate the car, and Tesla has not shown a maintenance price on the order screen.

Read that again: the subscription is not optional. The order page says so in plain language — "Subscription and regular vehicle maintenance required to operate." Nothing in the screenshots says the $299 price is locked, either. It is a subscription, and it can change at any time.

There are more limits. This is an invitation-only, early-access release. Tesla has not said how it picks who gets invited, and there is no public order page — if you did not get an invite, you cannot just go to tesla.com and buy one. The car only works inside Tesla's Robotaxi service areas: eight cities in Texas and Florida — Austin, Dallas, Houston, Fort Worth, San Antonio, Miami, Orlando, and Tampa. Drive it outside those areas and it does not operate.

And do not buy one planning to make money with it. The order terms explicitly prohibit owners from using the vehicle for commercial purposes, according to The Verge's reading of the fine print. No Uber, no DoorDash, and no putting it on Tesla's own Robotaxi network. Tesla has also not confirmed that a personal Cybercab bought under this invite can ever be added to its ride-hailing network or used to earn money.

Add it up and the first year looks like this: $29,990 before taxes, plus a $1,000 non-refundable deposit, plus 11 paid months of the subscription at $299 — $3,289 — for a first-year total around $34,279 before taxes, registration, insurance, and charging. That is the real number, not the one on the poster.

There is one more thing hanging over it. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened an audit query shortly after the Cybercab entered commercial service as a robotaxi, and that review is still open.

Is the Cybercab a real product now? Yes — real orders, real deposits, real delivery estimates. Is it a car you can buy and drive anywhere, the way you buy a truck today? Not even close. It is a $30,000 car that only runs in eight cities, only with a $299 monthly subscription, and only if Tesla invites you to buy it. That is worth knowing before the hype does your thinking for you.
