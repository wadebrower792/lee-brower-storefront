# AI Is Not Better Than Your Doctor. The Doctors' Own Association Said So.

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 10, 2026

On September 29, 2026, at a health summit in Washington, the Secretary of Health and Human Services made a big claim. He said AI could read a long medical record and give you a second opinion "much better informed than any doctor in the country."

That's a big claim. And it's wrong.

Here's what the doctors themselves say. The American Medical Association — the largest doctors' organization in the country — adopted new policy on June 10, 2026. Their CEO, Dr. John Whyte, said it in plain words: "AI has enormous potential in healthcare, but it cannot replace physician judgment."

Read that again. Cannot replace physician judgment. Not "not yet." Cannot.

The AMA's policy is that AI should be an assistive tool, not an autonomous decision-maker. A doctor has to stay in the loop, with transparency, accountability, and real oversight. That's not a suggestion from a skeptic. That's the official position of the profession.

Now, the numbers behind the claim tell their own story. The AMA surveyed nearly 1,700 physicians in 2026. Eighty-one percent said they use AI in their practice. That sounds like a lot — until you look at what they're using it for. They're using it for summaries, paperwork, discharge instructions, and billing codes. Only 17 percent used AI for assistive diagnosis. Using a tool to handle paperwork is not the same as the tool being a better doctor than a doctor.

And here's the part the summit claim left out: nobody has run the comparison. There is no study showing a general AI system beating doctors across real patients, real conditions, and real decisions. A system that can summarize a long record fast may help surface information. That doesn't mean it understands your condition better or picks the right treatment. Summarizing is not diagnosing.

So what does this mean for a working person? It means this: use AI to get ready for your doctor's visit. Ask it to explain a term in plain language. Ask it to help you write down questions. Then bring all of it to a real doctor and let the doctor decide. A chatbot cannot examine you. It doesn't know your whole history unless you type it in. And when it's wrong — and it will be wrong sometimes, with the same calm confidence as when it's right — nobody is responsible. There's no license on the line. There's no malpractice. There's just you, living with the answer.

Your health is not a place to test a slogan.
