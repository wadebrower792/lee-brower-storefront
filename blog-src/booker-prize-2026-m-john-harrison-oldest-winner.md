# At 81, M. John Harrison Just Won the Booker Prize — the Oldest Winner Ever

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 10, 2026

British author M. John Harrison won the 2026 Booker Prize on Monday night, November 9, for his novel *The End of Everything*. At 81 years old, he is the oldest author ever to win the prize — passing Margaret Atwood, who was 79 when she shared the award in 2019 for *The Testaments*. He takes home £50,000, about $67,000, and the Booker trophy.

The announcement came at a ceremony at Old Billingsgate in London, a former fish market on the Thames. The judging panel, chaired by historian Mary Beard, picked the book unanimously. The panel also included poet Raymond Antrobus, musician Jarvis Cocker of the band Pulp, critic Rebecca Liu, and novelist Patricia Lockwood. They chose from 163 submitted books, narrowed to a 13-book longlist and then six finalists in September.

This was Harrison's first Booker nomination — and his first win — in a writing career that spans six decades. He has been publishing science fiction and speculative fiction since 1971. *The End of Everything* is his 13th novel. It is also the first Booker win for his publisher, Serpent's Tail, an imprint of the independent press Profile Books, after five nominations.

## The book itself

*The End of Everything* is a dystopian satire set on England's Kent coast in the near future, years after social order has broken down. It follows a beachcomber named Phillip Tennent and his pistol-toting elderly aunt Marnie as they try to get by amid the arrival of mysterious shapeshifting entities called the iGhetti. The publisher calls it "a slyly satirical and unsettling post-apocalyptic adventure into the limits of human understanding."

At 198 pages, it was the shortest book on this year's shortlist. The judges said it left them with "a twinge of optimism" about the world. It is also widely described as the first science-fiction novel ever to win the Booker — a prize that, for most of its history, has gone to literary fiction. Harrison is the first winner to come out of the science-fiction world proper.

## Why this one matters

There is a lesson in this win that has nothing to do with books. Harrison is 81. He has been writing for sixty years. He was not the young hotshot on the list — the shortlist included two former winners, Marlon James and Douglas Stuart, and the bestselling Elizabeth Strout. He was the old hand with the shortest book and the strangest story, and the judges picked him unanimously.

That is how a career is supposed to work. You do the work for decades, you get better at it, and one day the work speaks for itself. Nobody handed Harrison this prize because he was young or fashionable. He got it because after sixty years, he wrote a book the judges could not put down.

Mary Beard, the chair of the judges, was clear that this was not a lifetime achievement award. Harrison is, in her words, only ten years older than she is. "We are not celebrating octogenarians," she said. The prize went to the book, not the birthday.

For anybody out there who thinks they started too late, or that their best work is behind them, or that the world only rewards the young — an 81-year-old just won the biggest prize in English fiction with his 13th novel. Keep working. The book is the thing.
