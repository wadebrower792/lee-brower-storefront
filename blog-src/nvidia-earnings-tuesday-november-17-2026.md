# Nvidia Reports Tuesday — Its First Earnings Without Jensen Huang

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 16, 2026

Tuesday is the biggest earnings day left in 2026, and it comes with a twist nobody saw coming a week ago. Nvidia reports its fiscal third-quarter results Tuesday, November 17, after the market closes — the first time in 33 years the company will report without Jensen Huang at the helm.

Huang resigned Thursday, November 12, as president, chief executive, and board member, effective immediately. Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO while keeping her job as CFO, and the board is searching for a permanent successor from inside and outside the company. Longtime director Harvey C. Jones was appointed as the board's first-ever chair. Kress will lead Tuesday's earnings call, her first as interim CEO.

Here are the numbers everybody will be watching. Results come out at about 4:20 p.m. Eastern, with the call at 5 p.m. Analysts polled by LSEG expect revenue of about $109.2 billion — roughly 91 percent higher than the same quarter a year ago — and adjusted earnings of about $2.48 a share. Nvidia's own guidance, given in August, was revenue of $108 billion, plus or minus 2 percent, so the consensus sits just above the company's own midpoint. The company also guided gross margin to 74 percent, down from 75 percent last quarter.

One thing worth understanding: the quarter being reported ended October 25, before Huang resigned. So the numbers themselves describe a business still run by its longtime leader. What the numbers can't tell you is what comes next. That's why the guidance for the current quarter — the first set under Kress — and what she says on the call will matter more than the reported quarter. For the quarter ending in January, analysts expect about $124.9 billion in revenue, according to Yahoo Finance.

The market has been jumpy all week. Options traders expect Nvidia's stock to swing about 9.1 percent in either direction on the report. And the shakeup has already rippled across the Pacific: on Monday, Taiwan's Taiex index fell 6.03 percent, its second-largest point drop ever, with TSMC down 5.74 percent. South Korea's Kospi lost 3.59 percent, and SK hynix fell 7.89 percent. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said over the weekend that Nvidia needs a "dramatic shakeup" with a CEO from outside the company, and wants answers on export compliance within 10 days.

None of that changes what Tuesday actually is: a report card on the company at the center of the AI buildout, delivered by a new voice, in the middle of a leadership search. Morningstar senior equity analyst Brian Colello said he expects "another beat-and-raise quarter" and that a miss or a weak forecast would surprise him, given how much cloud providers and enterprises are still spending.

For folks outside the stock market, here's the plain version. The company that makes the chips powering nearly all AI work just lost the man who built it, and tomorrow it has to show the world the business is bigger than one person. The numbers will probably be strong — they were set in motion months ago. The real question is the outlook, and whether the people running the company now can keep the trust of the customers buying those chips by the billions.

No predictions here. Just the facts, and a Tuesday worth watching.
