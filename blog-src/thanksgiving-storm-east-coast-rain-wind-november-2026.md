# A Thanksgiving Storm Is Coming to the East Coast — Rain, 50 mph Winds, and Flooding

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 23, 2026

If you're driving or flying anywhere along the East Coast for Thanksgiving, the weather has plans of its own. The National Weather Service says a powerful coastal storm is headed for the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast, bringing heavy rain, high winds, and coastal flooding right across the holiday — Wednesday through Saturday, with the worst of the rain landing on Thanksgiving Day itself.

Here's what the Weather Prediction Center put out early Monday morning, November 23: confidence is high for an impactful holiday storm, with heavy rain, high winds, and coastal impacts for the East Coast. The heavy rain threat is mainly for Thanksgiving Day along the Mid-Atlantic and Northeast coasts. The Carolinas and the Mid-Atlantic are looking at 1 to 4 inches of rain, and the Northeast 1 to 2 inches. There's also a broad marginal risk of excessive rainfall — at least a 5 percent chance — running from the Carolina and Virginia Tidewater all the way up through southern New England on Thursday.

The wind is the other half of it. Gusts of 50 mph or more are forecast along the coast, with 40 mph or more inland into the I-95 corridor, and the strong winds will stick around through at least Friday night. The Weather Service says power outages and tree damage are possible. Strong onshore winds, high surf, and high astronomical tides could also bring significant coastal flooding and beach erosion from Georgia up through the Northeast.

Some wintry weather is mixed in too, mainly in the higher elevations and inland areas, though the headline for most folks is rain and wind, not snow.

The timing could hardly be worse for travel. Thanksgiving week is the busiest travel period in the country, and the storm hits the East Coast exactly when millions of people are on the road or in the air — Wednesday out, Sunday and Monday back. The Weather Service's own advice is simple: those traveling should monitor National Weather Service forecasts and heed guidance from local officials.

For folks in the trades, this is also the kind of week that tests a job site. High wind and standing water will shut down outdoor work along the coast, and any job with material staged outside, open trenches, or temporary coverings needs to be buttoned up before Wednesday. A 50 mph gust doesn't care about your schedule. Tie it down, cover it up, or bring it inside — and don't leave a ladder or a sheet of plywood where the wind can turn it into a projectile.

If you're traveling, the smart moves are the same ones every year, just more important this time: leave early, keep the tank full, check your route and your flight status before you leave the house, and build in a buffer. If your flight connects through the Northeast on Thursday, know that delays can cascade fast when the wind is howling and the rain is coming sideways.

Thanksgiving is about getting home to the people you love. The storm will pass — the rain and wind ease off as the system moves into the Canadian Maritimes by the weekend. Give yourself the time to get there safe, and let the weather do what it's going to do.
