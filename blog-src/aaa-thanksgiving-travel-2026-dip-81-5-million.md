# Thanksgiving Travel Dips for the First Time in Three Years — 81.5 Million Still on the Move

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 18, 2026

Thanksgiving travel is finally cooling off — just barely. AAA projects 81.5 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles from home for Thanksgiving between Tuesday, November 24, and Monday, November 30. That's about 121,000 fewer travelers than last year's 81.6 million, a dip of 0.1%.

That small number ends a three-year streak of record Thanksgiving travel that started in 2023. And as AAA's travel vice president Stacey Barber put it, "travelers won't notice much of a difference on the roads and at the airports, since projected volumes remain near record levels."

Here's how the 81.5 million breaks down by how people get there. AAA projects 72.9 million people will drive, down from 73.1 million last year — a drop of about 200,000 road-trippers that AAA ties in part to this season's record-high gas prices. Regular gasoline averaged $3.82 a gallon on Sunday, November 15, compared with $3.08 a year earlier, according to AAA.

Flying is down a little too. AAA projects 5.96 million travelers will take domestic flights, a 0.2% decrease from last year, and the average domestic round-trip flight booked through AAA costs $800, up 17% from the same holiday period in 2025. The one mode going up: buses, trains, and cruises, with 2.6 million Americans expected to use them, a 4.1% increase from last year.

If you're driving, timing matters. INRIX, the traffic-data company AAA works with, expects Tuesday and Wednesday afternoons to be the busiest on the roads as people head out, and Sunday and Monday as they come home. Morning departures are the better bet.

One caveat on the forecast: AAA issued it on Monday, November 16, and it was prepared the week of October 12 — before the FAA imposed a nationwide ground stop on Friday, November 13, after communications and radar problems. That stop lasted more than four hours. AAA said it does not expect the disruption to significantly affect holiday travel unless similar problems happen again.

The bottom line for families: the roads and airports will still be packed. The dip is real, but it's the difference between a record crowd and a near-record crowd. Leave early, check your route, and give yourself more time than you think you need.
