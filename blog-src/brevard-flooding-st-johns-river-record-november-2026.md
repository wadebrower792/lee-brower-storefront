# The St. Johns River Just Broke a 73-Year-Old Record — and Florida's Space Coast Is Underwater

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 19, 2026

The St. Johns River near Cocoa, Florida, topped 18 feet early this morning, breaking a record that had stood since October 1953. The old record was 17.6 feet. Forecasters had not expected the river to reach even 17.4 feet until Saturday afternoon. By 1 p.m. today it was expected to reach 18.2 feet and stay near that level through Friday afternoon.

That matters because 17.2 feet is the level at which forecasters warn river water can start entering homes in neighborhoods in west Cocoa and Rockledge. The river blew past that mark before sunrise.

Brevard County has been hammered by rain. Storms dumped between 6 and 10 inches over 12 hours overnight, with some pockets getting even more. The Satellite Beach area saw almost 12 inches on Wednesday alone. The average across the county for Wednesday was 3.4 inches, according to the St. Johns River Water Management District's rainfall radar. The National Weather Service in Melbourne expects another 1 to 3 inches today, with a 40 to 60 percent chance of another 3 to 6 inches along the east-central Florida coast through Saturday.

The flooding is real and happening right now. A flash flood warning stayed in effect through 9:45 a.m. for central Brevard County, including Merritt Island, Cocoa, Cape Canaveral, and Rockledge, and the weather service called the flash flooding "life-threatening." Emergency crews have already carried out at least two water rescues — one in Cocoa and one in Rockledge — plus at least four well-being checks overnight. Melbourne police reported cars stuck in floodwaters by 8:30 a.m., with significant flooding at Commodore Boulevard and Aurora Road. State Road A1A is flooded in places, including outside Patrick Space Force Base and in Cocoa Beach.

Brevard Public Schools are closed today. Florida Tech closed its offices and cancelled classes, as did Eastern Florida State College. Health First delayed opening its medical offices until noon and suspended elective procedures at Parrish Healthcare clinics. The State of the Port address at Port Canaveral was postponed. The Space Force issued a strong wind warning until 4 p.m. for Kennedy Space Center and Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, with 25-knot winds gusting to 35 knots.

And it may not be over. The National Hurricane Center is tracking a system off Florida that has a medium chance of strengthening into a subtropical or tropical storm. That system has already been pulling moisture from hundreds of miles out in the Atlantic into Florida.

For anybody in construction, this is the kind of week that tests everything: job sites under water, roads closed, crews that cannot get to work, and materials sitting in standing water. The plain rules still apply. Do not drive through floodwater or around barricades — Melbourne city officials said exactly that this morning. Do not wade into water you cannot see the bottom of. Document damage with photos before you touch anything, because insurance and clients will both want the record. And if you are a homeowner in the area, stay off the roads if you can. Brevard County Emergency Management put it plainly: "If you don't have to get on the roads, don't."

Florida gets storms. But a river breaking a record set in 1953, two days ahead of forecast, is the kind of thing worth paying attention to — whether you live on the Space Coast or you are just watching the weather from Alabama.
