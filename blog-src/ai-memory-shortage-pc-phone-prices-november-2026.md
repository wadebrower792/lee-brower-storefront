# AI Is Eating the World's Memory — and Your Next Laptop Is Paying for It

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 4, 2026

If you priced a computer, a phone, or a RAM upgrade this year and thought the number looked wrong, it was not your imagination. The memory chips inside those devices have gotten dramatically more expensive in 2026, and the buyer outbidding you is not another phone maker. It is the AI data center industry — the same buildout that is putting up data centers across the country, including the ones contractors are being hired to build.

## What actually happened to prices

The numbers are stark, and they come from the firms that track this market for a living. TrendForce surveys reported conventional DRAM contract prices rising roughly 90 percent or more in the first quarter of 2026 alone, with another large increase expected in the second quarter. A survey by SigmaIntel found the 12-gigabyte memory package used in phones went from $77.10 to $145.90 in a single quarter — an 89 percent jump, on top of a first quarter in which prices had already roughly doubled. On the retail side, a 32-gigabyte kit of Corsair desktop memory that sold for about $100 last October was selling for about $440 by June, according to reporting that pulled those figures together.

The device makers have passed it on. Dell raised prices on commercial laptops, desktops, and monitors by 10 to 30 percent starting December 17, 2025, citing memory costs. Asus raised prices in Taiwan, with resellers putting mainstream laptop increases at 15 to 20 percent in January. In May, Valve raised the Steam Deck OLED from $549 to $789, citing rising memory and storage costs. Those are reported company moves.

## Why AI gets the chips first

The mechanism is straightforward. AI accelerators — the chips at the center of every data center Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, and Alphabet are building — need high-bandwidth memory, or HBM, made by stacking DRAM dies beside the processor. Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron control more than 90 percent of global DRAM production, and they have redirected manufacturing capacity toward HBM, where margins are far higher than on consumer memory. One widely cited projection puts data center spending by those four companies at about $650 billion this year, up from $217 billion in 2024, and data centers consuming roughly 70 percent of the world's memory output in 2026. SK Hynix reportedly sold out its entire 2026 production and stopped taking orders.

## Forecasts disagree — read them as forecasts

Gartner's February 2026 forecast projected combined DRAM and SSD prices surging 130 percent by the end of the year, pushing average PC prices up 17 percent and smartphone prices up 13 percent, with PC shipments falling 10.4 percent and smartphone shipments falling 8.4 percent. IDC's estimates, reported by TechSpot, are more moderate — PC prices rising by up to 8 percent if the shortage persists, with budget phone brands most exposed. The gap between those two firms is the point: these are projections, not settled facts, and they weight AI demand growth and supply expansion differently. Counterpoint researchers told TechCrunch that elevated phone prices are likely to last until at least the end of 2027, though the pace of increases should slow.

## What it means on a real job

For a contractor, the lesson is practical. The laptop that runs your estimating software, the tablet that holds your plan set, and the phone in your pocket are all carrying a memory surcharge now, and last year's price is not a quote for this year's machine. If a device is due for replacement, price the whole build before you commit, check whether a memory or storage upgrade is what is actually driving the quote, and do not assume the entry-level model will still be there at the entry-level price — Gartner's forecast says the sub-$500 PC segment disappears by 2028. That is a forecast, not a promise, but the direction of every reported price move this year agrees with it.

The AI boom is creating construction work and raising the price of the tools other workers buy, both from the same cause.
