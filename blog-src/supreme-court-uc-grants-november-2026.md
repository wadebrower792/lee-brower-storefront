# The Supreme Court Said No to Reviving $59 Million in UC Grant Cuts

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 17, 2026

The U.S. Supreme Court handed the Trump administration a setback on Monday, November 16, refusing to revive about $59 million in research grants it had cut from University of California researchers. The justices declined to hear the case, gave no explanation, and no justice publicly noted a dissent. The lower court rulings stand — and the money keeps flowing while the case continues.

Here's what happened, step by step. In early 2025, after President Donald Trump signed executive orders targeting diversity, equity, and inclusion programs, the National Science Foundation and the National Endowment for the Humanities terminated grants to UC researchers. The agencies said the work violated the executive order because the research was perceived to express DEI or environmental-justice viewpoints. The grants covered a wide range of work — climate policy, water management, medicine, U.S. history, disease research, and biology, among others.

Researchers at UC Berkeley and across the University of California sued. Their case is called Trump v. Thakur. U.S. District Judge Rita Lin ordered the agencies to reinstate the grants in June 2025. On May 26, 2026, a panel of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the 9th Circuit upheld that order, ruling the researchers were likely to win on a First Amendment claim — that the government had singled out research because of the viewpoints it expressed. The administration asked the Supreme Court to step in and undo that outcome. On Monday, the Court said no.

Two things the decision does not do. It does not settle the constitutional question. A denial of review is not a ruling on the merits, and it creates no nationwide precedent. The underlying case goes back to Judge Lin's court in the Northern District of California, where the researchers are still pursuing a final resolution. And it does not end the fight over every grant. The $59 million at issue here is the slice tied to NSF and NEH grants terminated over perceived DEI viewpoints, with some awards scheduled to run as late as November 2029. A separate challenge over grants ended without a stated reason is pending in the Court of Federal Claims.

The bigger picture is worth knowing. Between January and July of 2025, the White House terminated more than $900 million in grants to UC researchers overall, including nearly $600 million at UCLA alone, according to reporting on the case. The $59 million in this Supreme Court case is the part the administration specifically asked the justices to rule on. The Court's refusal leaves that money funded and leaves the First Amendment barrier in place — for now.

This was one of three DEI-related cases the Court turned away on Monday, along with disputes over medical training requirements and speech at a state legislature. None of those denials resolves the underlying constitutional questions. What they do is leave the lower court rulings in force while the cases grind on — which, for the UC researchers and their labs, means the work continues and the paychecks keep coming.
