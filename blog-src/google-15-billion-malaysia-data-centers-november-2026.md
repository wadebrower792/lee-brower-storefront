# Google Is Spending $15 Billion on Data Centers in Malaysia — and Contractors Should Be Watching

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 9, 2026

Google announced on November 4 that it will invest $15 billion in Malaysia over the next two years, 2027 and 2028, to build new data center campuses and the power, water, and training that go with them. It is one of the biggest private digital infrastructure commitments ever made in the country, and it is the latest proof of something every working contractor already feels in his bones: the data center buildout is not slowing down, it is spreading.

The plan was unveiled in the presence of Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim. Google's first Malaysian campus, at Elmina Business Park in Selangor, is now operational. New campuses are planned in Selangor and Negeri Sembilan, and the Selangor capacity will support Google's upcoming Google Cloud region in Malaysia — the local home for the cloud and AI services used by companies like AirAsia, Gamuda, Hong Leong Bank, and Maxis, along with the Ministry of Digital and the Ministry of Higher Education.

This is not just a building job. Google says it will help add more than 1 gigawatt of clean energy capacity to Malaysia's national grid, working with the utility Tenaga Nasional Berhad. It also announced $160 million on November 2 for an off-river reservoir along the Linggi River and a water treatment plant in Negeri Sembilan, because data centers drink water as well as power. Google says it will pay for all the electricity its campuses use and the direct infrastructure costs of connecting them to the grid.

The company also says its campuses run at a Power Usage Effectiveness of 1.12 to 1.14 in tropical climates like Malaysia's, against an industry average of 1.54 — and that water cooling cuts energy use by about 10 percent compared with air cooling. Those are Google's own figures, and a contractor should read them as the new bar: efficiency is now part of the spec, not a nice extra.

## What it means for the trades

Google says the 2027–2028 construction phase will contribute an average of $1.1 billion a year to Malaysia's GDP and support more than 37,000 local jobs a year, including roughly 6,000 in construction, with average annual labor income of $378 million. Those are company projections, not independently audited numbers — but the direction is plain. The company is already working with a domestic supply chain of more than 80 Malaysian entities across construction and civil engineering, network infrastructure, power and cooling components, logistics, legal, catering, and waste management.

Four new training programs aim to upskill about 800,000 Malaysians by 2031, and Google is setting up Data Center Institutes at selected polytechnics in Selangor and Negeri Sembilan starting in 2027, to train people for technical roles in data center deployment and operations.

This commitment builds on a $2 billion digital infrastructure investment Google announced in 2024 and a presence in Malaysia of more than 15 years. Malaysia's Digital Ministry has already invited Google to collaborate on AI Cities work beyond the data centers.

For a contractor in Alabama or Florida, the lesson is not about Malaysia. It is about the pattern. Big tech is putting real money into power, water, and skilled labor everywhere it builds — and the companies that can do the civil work, the electrical work, and the cooling work are the ones getting called. The data center wave is not a news story. It is a job board.
