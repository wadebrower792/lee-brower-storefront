# Microsoft's Email Went Down Worldwide — and It's Still Not Fully Back

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 13, 2026

If your email stopped working yesterday, it wasn't your phone and it wasn't your internet. Microsoft's email system went down around the world on Thursday afternoon, November 12, and as of this morning it still isn't fully back.

Outlook users couldn't send or receive email or even load their inboxes. Teams, SharePoint, and Microsoft's AI assistant Copilot went down with it. The outage tracker Downdetector logged about 130,000 Outlook problem reports in the United States around 1 p.m. Eastern on Thursday, with reports coming in from 74 countries within about an hour. Microsoft 365 reports topped 47,000 on top of that.

This wasn't a small glitch in one office. In Australia, the country's spy agency and its federal police were both hit — their leaders confirmed it at a news conference. India's airlines IndiGo and Akasa Air said their customer-support email was disrupted. Singapore's government technology agency said government emails were down. In the United States, industrial company Workhorse Group delayed a quarterly earnings call because of the outage.

Microsoft says it has no indication the outage was caused by a cyberattack or any malicious activity. What the company has said so far is that some servers became unexpectedly unavailable after failing automated health checks, which put elevated load on the rest of the system. The company paused deployments, moved traffic off the affected infrastructure, and started restarting servers. Its first fixes, Microsoft admitted, did not work "as expected."

By Friday morning, Microsoft reported progress. The company said Teams service health and functionality had been restored, and email delivery was improving across a growing portion of the affected infrastructure. But it also said plainly that capacity "has not yet fully returned to baseline" and gave no estimated time for full restoration. To keep delayed messages from being lost, Microsoft extended its email timeout window to 48 hours.

Microsoft is tracking the incident under two numbers on its service health page: EX1550543 for the Exchange Online mailbox problem and MO1550821 for the wider Microsoft 365 disruption. Other services caught up in it include Microsoft Defender XDR, Intune, Planner, and Purview.

Australia's Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke said Microsoft had likened the scale of this outage to the CrowdStrike outage of 2024 — the one that grounded flights and shut down hospitals around the world. That comparison tells you how big this is.

For a contractor or a small business owner, the lesson is simple and it costs nothing to learn: don't build your whole business on one company's inbox. If your bids, your change orders, and your client messages all live in one email account, one bad Thursday can freeze your whole operation. Keep a second way to reach your clients — a phone number, a text thread, a backup email on a different service. The cloud is convenient right up until the day it isn't.
