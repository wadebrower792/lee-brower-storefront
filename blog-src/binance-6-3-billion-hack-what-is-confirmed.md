# Binance Hack: $6.3 Billion Gone, and What Is Actually Confirmed

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, October 30, 2026

On October 29, 2026, hackers drained about $6.3 billion from Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, in what is being described as the biggest crypto theft on record. The numbers are still moving as firms trace the money, so this post sticks to what Binance and the tracing firms have actually said, and labels the rest as attribution, not proven fact.

## What happened, as reported

Binance said its estimated total impact sits at around $6.3 billion, and that it has worked with blockchain partners to freeze about $3 billion of affected assets on-chain so far. Most of the theft happened in about 20 minutes, according to Reuters, starting late Thursday, October 29. Binance temporarily suspended deposits and withdrawals to run security checks and trace funds, and said it expects withdrawal services to resume within 48 hours. Binance also said it will cover all losses from company funds and an emergency assets fund, adding that user funds are safe. That is Binance's pledge, stated as its pledge, not a result that has already happened.

Binance's preliminary investigation points to a compromised laptop belonging to a DevOps engineer, which let an attacker reach a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions. Binance said it found no evidence of any wallet private key leak. The full attack path is not yet confirmed, and the investigation is ongoing.

## Who is blamed, and by whom

Blockchain tracing firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis attributed the heist to North Korea's TraderTraitor hacking unit, with TRM saying it made that judgment with high confidence based on on- and off-chain intelligence. Elliptic's analysis put the stolen total at $6.43 billion and also attributed the theft to North Korea, noting some funds reached an address tied to proceeds of a suspected North Korean exploit of Bitget. Binance itself has not named anyone, and North Korea has previously denied hacking allegations. The honest way to state this is: tracing firms attribute it to North Korea; Binance has not. Those are two different statements, and this post does not merge them.

For scale, the previous record was the Bybit hack in February 2025, in which attackers took almost $1.5 billion. The Binance theft is more than four times that figure. Bitcoin fell to a six-week low after the news, last down about 3 percent at $76,469.82 according to Reuters.

## What this means for a working person

This post is not investment advice, and it does not tell anyone to buy, sell, or hold anything. The plain lesson is about custody and single points of failure: an exchange's own systems signed the withdrawals, and the entry point was one engineer's laptop, according to Binance's own preliminary findings. Any system where one compromised machine can move billions is a system worth understanding before trusting it with your money. Figures in this post reflect the snapshots reported on October 30, 2026, and will change as more funds are frozen or traced.
