# Godzilla Just Broke a 22-Year-Old Box Office Record — With Subtitles

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 9, 2026

Godzilla Minus Zero stomped into North American movie theaters this weekend and walked out with $31.6 million, topping the box office and setting a new record for the biggest opening weekend ever for a live-action foreign-language film. The old record belonged to the 2002 Chinese film Hero, which opened with $17.8 million — and it had stood for more than two decades.

The film, directed by Takashi Yamazaki and distributed in North America by GKIDS, opened in 3,254 theaters. It is the sequel to 2023's Godzilla Minus One, which won the Oscar for best visual effects and became the highest-grossing Japanese live-action film ever in the United States and Canada. The sequel nearly tripled its predecessor's $11.4 million opening, and it did it with a budget of less than $20 million.

Worldwide, Godzilla Minus Zero took in $63 million over the weekend. In Japan, where it opened November 3 — the anniversary of the 1954 original — it earned about $5.75 million on its first day, a record for a live-action film's opening day in Japan and more than double what the first film made on its opening day there.

## The fine print on the record

Every record has an asterisk, and this one is honest about it. The Passion of the Christ opened with $83.8 million in 2004 despite its largely Aramaic dialogue, and last year's animated Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle debuted with $70.6 million. The $31.6 million record is specifically for a live-action foreign-language film — and within that honest frame, it is still a genuine milestone. It is also the fourth non-English film ever to reach No. 1 at the U.S. weekend box office, and GKIDS's biggest opening ever, passing the $13 million it set in 2023 with The Boy and the Heron.

Audiences liked what they saw. The film earned an A CinemaScore and sits at 90 percent on Rotten Tomatoes. Large-format screens did heavy lifting: $21.3 million of the worldwide total — about 34 percent — came from IMAX. The runner-up this weekend, Warner Bros.' animated The Cat in the Hat, opened with $20.5 million.

The wider box office had a strong weekend too. North American theaters collected an estimated $121.6 million, the strongest first weekend of November since the pandemic, up 45 percent on the same weekend last year. Total domestic box office earnings for 2026 have reached $8.6 billion as of November 8, up 20 percent from the same period in 2025.

What makes this one worth noting is not just the monster. It is the proof that a Japanese-language film, shown with English subtitles, can outdraw everything Hollywood released the same weekend — on a budget smaller than most studio comedies. The audience showed up for a story done well, in the language it was made in. That is a lesson any builder can respect: do the work right, and people will come to you.
