# Cyclone Murasu Is Headed for India's Andhra Coast Tonight

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 2, 2026

A severe cyclonic storm named Murasu is bearing down on India's east coast, and landfall is expected tonight. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) says the storm will cross the south Andhra Pradesh coast between Nellore and Narsapur, close to Ongole, between late tonight, November 2, and the early hours of November 3, as a severe cyclonic storm with sustained winds of 100 to 110 kilometers per hour and gusts up to 120.

At 5:30 a.m. India time on November 2, the storm sat about 200 kilometers east-northeast of Chennai, 210 kilometers east-southeast of Nellore, and 260 kilometers southeast of Ongole, moving west-northwest. The IMD's November 1 bulletin had it about 480 kilometers east-southeast of Chennai a day earlier, so the storm has been closing on the coast steadily.

## What the warnings say

The IMD has put out a storm surge warning for coastal south Andhra Pradesh: a surge of about 1.0 to 1.5 meters above normal tide levels is likely to flood low-lying areas at landfall. Great Danger Signal No. X has been raised for the ports of Nizampatnam and Vadarevu. Heavy rain is forecast across coastal Andhra Pradesh and Yanam from November 2 to 4, with Rayalaseema getting rain November 2 to 3, and Telangana, Odisha, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Kerala all in the rainfall picture through the week.

Andhra Pradesh authorities planned to evacuate about 30,000 people ahead of the storm, with roughly 2,000 already moved by November 2. Bangladesh's four seaports — Chattogram, Cox's Bazar, Mongla, and Payra — were told to display distant warning signal number two as the storm moved through the west-central Bay of Bengal.

## The second storm in a week

Murasu is the second cyclonic storm to threaten India's east coast in less than seven days. Cyclone Arnab hit Andhra Pradesh and neighboring Odisha on October 28, 2026, and reporting says more than 180,000 people were evacuated across the two states during preparations for that storm. Arnab killed six people and destroyed houses and crops, according to the same reporting — which is why officials are moving early on Murasu even though it is forecast to be the weaker of the two.

That back-to-back pattern is the part worth watching. One storm saturates the ground and fills the rivers; the second one arrives before anything has dried out. The IMD's rainfall forecast for Odisha — isolated very heavy rainfall on November 3 and 4 — shows the risk extends well beyond the landfall point. A storm's damage is not just wind at the coast; it is rain falling on ground that is already full.

## What to watch next

The landfall window is narrow — late tonight into early tomorrow morning, India time — and the IMD updates its bulletins through the day. If you have family, crews, or business in coastal Andhra Pradesh or Tamil Nadu, the time to check on them is now, before the storm crosses, not after. The forecast track, the wind speeds, and the surge warning are all in the IMD's own bulletins, and those are the numbers to trust over social media estimates.

Storms do not care about schedules. The people who come through them best are the ones who moved early, stayed off the roads during landfall, and waited for the official all-clear instead of guessing when it was safe. Murasu will be over land by tomorrow morning. The rain, the flooding, and the cleanup will take longer.
