# Norris Wins in the Rain at São Paulo — and Mercedes Clinches the Constructors' Title

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 9, 2026

Lando Norris won a wet São Paulo Grand Prix on Sunday, November 8, driving his McLaren from pole position to victory at Interlagos and beating Max Verstappen by 14.602 seconds. Isack Hadjar finished third in the other Red Bull. It was Norris's third win of the season, his 14th career victory, and his 50th podium — and his second straight win in Brazil.

The race was a weather fight from start to finish. Rain soaked the track at the start, Norris lost the lead to new world champion Kimi Antonelli off the line and then dropped to third when Liam Lawson swept around his outside at the first turn. Norris judged the crossover from wet tires to slicks perfectly, slashed a seven-second gap, and forced Antonelli into a spin at Turn 1. When the rain returned for the final nine laps, he was already in control, leading 47 of the race's 71 laps. Brazil soccer great Neymar waved the checkered flag.

The bigger prize on the day went to Mercedes. The team clinched its ninth Formula One constructors' world championship — its first since 2021 — with three races still to run. Mercedes needed only three more points than Ferrari to seal it, and the Ferraris never threatened: Charles Leclerc finished seventh and Lewis Hamilton ninth. George Russell came home fourth and Antonelli fifth, and that was more than enough. Mercedes now has 652 points against Ferrari's 510 and cannot be caught.

## A title double, five years in the making

The constructors' crown completes a title double for Mercedes. Antonelli clinched the drivers' championship a week earlier in Mexico City at 20 years and 68 days, the youngest champion in Formula 1 history. Mercedes's ninth constructors' title equals Williams for third on the all-time list, behind McLaren on 10 and Ferrari on 16. It is the team's first constructors' title since the end of its eight-year run from 2014 to 2021, after four years of underperformance behind Red Bull and then McLaren.

Team principal Toto Wolff celebrated his ninth constructors' title with the team, and the double brings him to a combined 17 championships, equaling Ron Dennis at the top of the team-principal leaderboard.

For Norris, the win is a statement about momentum. He is the 2025 world champion, and after losing the drivers' crown to a 20-year-old this year, he is finishing the season the way a champion should — winning races, in the rain, from pole, against the best in the world. McLaren brought another upgrade to Brazil, and Norris made it count.

The season now heads to its final stretch. The next race is the Las Vegas Grand Prix on November 21, followed by Qatar and Abu Dhabi — though the last two carry some uncertainty because of tensions in the Middle East. Interlagos, for its part, is locked in: the track will stay on the Formula 1 calendar until at least 2035.
