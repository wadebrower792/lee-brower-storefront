# "Bitcoin to Zero" Searches Spiked 450 Percent. Here's What the Market Actually Did

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 2, 2026

Google searches for "Bitcoin to zero" jumped 450 percent in 24 hours this weekend, with more than 1.5 million searches, as the crypto market's rough year got rougher. That search spike is a mood reading, not a market forecast. The numbers underneath it are worth a straight look, because they tell a calmer story than the panic suggests — and a worse one than the optimists want.

## What the market did

Total crypto market value fell from about $2.94 trillion on October 1 to roughly $2.60 trillion by the end of October, a monthly loss of nearly $340 billion, according to CoinGecko data reported this week. That pushed total year-to-date losses to around $480 billion. Bitcoin itself fell from an opening level of about $87,848 at the start of the year to near $77,424 by October 31 — down roughly 12 percent over ten months. Bitcoin closed last week at $78,311, down 2.17 percent on the week, and was trading near $77,946 on Monday morning.

Those are real declines, but they are not a market going to zero. A market that lost $480 billion in ten months still holds about $2.6 trillion. Bitcoin at $78,000 is a long way from zero, and it is still roughly 60 percent of the total crypto market. The panic search trend and the actual price chart are measuring two different things: fear, and value.

## Why it fell

The reporting points to a pile-up rather than a single cause. Tariff threats and geopolitical tension pushed investors out of risky assets. Institutional money pulled back. And the market was already rattled by the Binance hack on October 29, when hackers drained about $6.3 billion from the exchange's hot wallets — the largest crypto theft on record. Binance said its own funds and its SAFU fund would cover the losses, and withdrawals reopened in phases, with first-phase withdrawals restored across 15 networks by the morning of November 1.

Leveraged traders took the worst of it. About $6.22 billion in crypto futures contracts were liquidated during the October slide, and realized losses for Bitcoin holders topped $1.1 billion over October 30–31 alone, according to CryptoQuant data, as short-term holders sold into the drop below $80,000.

## What the fear index says — and what it doesn't

The widely watched Fear & Greed Index sat at 37, or "Fear," at the end of last week. That is a sentiment gauge built from volatility, volume, social media, and surveys. It measures how people feel, not where prices go next. Extreme fear readings have historically shown up near market bottoms and near the middle of longer slides alike. It is a thermometer, not a compass.

One more thing the numbers show: this is not just a crypto story. October was a rough month for risk assets across the board. The Nasdaq had its worst month since March 2025, and big tech names took heavy hits the same week as the Binance hack. When portfolios need cash, liquid assets get sold first — and Bitcoin is one of the most liquid assets there is.

## The honest bottom line

This is not investment advice, and nothing here is a buy or sell call. What the verified numbers say is simple: crypto lost about $480 billion in market value this year, Bitcoin is down about 12 percent year to date, and search interest in "Bitcoin to zero" spiked because fear is loud right now. Markets have recovered from worse drawdowns before, and they have also kept falling after fear spikes. The search bar is not a price target. Watch the actual numbers — price, flows, and whether withdrawals and redemptions stay orderly — and ignore the noise in between.
