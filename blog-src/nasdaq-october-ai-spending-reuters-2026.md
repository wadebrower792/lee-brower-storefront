# The Nasdaq's $2 Trillion October: AI Spending Is the Question Now, Not the Answer

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 1, 2026

October ended with a number that stops you cold: more than $2 trillion in market value was set to be wiped from the tech-heavy Nasdaq 100 during the month, according to a Reuters report published October 30, 2026. At the time of that report, the index had already fallen more than 5.8 percent in October.

This is not investment advice, and it is not a buy or sell call. It is a plain reading of what earnings season actually showed — and why investors who happily funded the AI build-out all year started asking harder questions in October.

## The question changed

For most of the AI boom, the market's question was: how fast can these companies build? In October, the question became: when does the spending pay off, and how fast?

Reuters reported that Meta Platforms, Alphabet, and Amazon all raised their spending forecasts further as they race to expand AI infrastructure. That is the heart of it. The companies are not pulling back. They are spending more, faster — and investors are now weighing that spending against how quickly it turns into profit.

The report put it bluntly: Big Tech earnings intensified investor scrutiny of whether the billions being poured into artificial intelligence can generate returns quickly enough. Rising borrowing costs add to the worry, because debt-funded investment gets more expensive and harder to justify when the payoff timeline stretches out.

## What the earnings actually showed

Alphabet suffered its biggest one-day percentage drop on record on Thursday, October 29, after disappointing results in its core search advertising business and a massive acceleration in cash burn, Reuters reported. Meta's plans for a record $97.5 billion fund raise heightened concerns about shareholder dilution and whether mounting AI spending could force other big tech companies to increasingly tap capital markets.

Not every company got the same treatment. Microsoft and SpaceX broke from the gloom, Reuters said, with results that showed AI spending starting to pay off. Amazon's cloud business, Amazon Web Services, continued to accelerate, and Meta itself offered better-than-expected revenue growth. The revenue is real. The argument is about the cost of getting it and the speed of the return.

## The scale of the spending

The numbers behind the debate are staggering, and they come from the banks' own forecasts, not from headlines. Morgan Stanley forecast that capital spending on AI infrastructure overall will surpass a trillion dollars next year. J.P. Morgan expects global AI capital spending to reach $5.5 trillion by 2030, and sees $900 billion being spent in 2026 alone. J.P. Morgan analysts also noted that this year's AI spending by Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Oracle, and Microsoft would likely equal their combined spending since the November 2022 launch of ChatGPT.

Microsoft is reportedly planning to raise about $25 billion through its first U.S. investment-grade bond sale in more than nine years, Reuters reported, and Meta, Amazon, and Alphabet have issued hundreds of billions of dollars in bond sales over the past four years.

## What to watch next

Reuters noted the earnings calendar is not done: Apple was set to report the following week, and AI bellwether Nvidia was due in mid-November. Those two reports will either calm the October nerves or deepen them.

For a working person, the lesson is simpler than the market commentary. A boom built on spending eventually gets judged on returns. October was the month the market started doing that judging in earnest. Whether the AI build-out pays off on the schedule investors want is still an open question — and it is now the question that moves the market.
