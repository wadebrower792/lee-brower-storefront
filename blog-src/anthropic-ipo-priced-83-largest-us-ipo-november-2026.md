# Anthropic Priced Its IPO at $83 — the Biggest in U.S. History — and Starts Trading Today

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 19, 2026

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI assistant, priced its initial public offering at $83 a share on Wednesday evening, above the $70 to $78 range it had set. The shares start trading today, Thursday, November 19, on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT.

The numbers are enormous. The offering covers about 980 million shares, with total gross proceeds of approximately $81.3 billion — the largest IPO in U.S. history, according to Renaissance Capital. Anthropic itself is selling about 956 million of those shares, with existing shareholders selling the rest. The offering values the company at approximately $1.38 trillion on a fully diluted basis, according to Renaissance Capital's IPO analysis.

That puts a price tag on the AI race that everybody can now watch move in real time. Anthropic is OpenAI's biggest direct competitor, and its stock price from here on out will be a public scoreboard for whether the business of selling AI to companies can support a trillion-dollar valuation. The company reported revenue of $17.0 billion for the third quarter of 2026 — up more than twelvefold from a year earlier — with $33.2 billion for the first nine months of the year, according to reports citing company filings.

The timing could hardly be more pointed. OpenAI released two new models, GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol, earlier this week, on November 17. Astra is the flagship for demanding professional work; Sol is the cheaper option for everyday tasks and coding. But several early testers panned them. Investor Matt Shumer called the new models essentially unusable and declined to write his usual review, and developer Theo Browne called Astra the worst model OpenAI has released, according to VentureBeat. OpenAI's product team responded that the models changed substantially in the final days before launch.

Google is not sitting still either. It launched Gemini 4 Neon on November 18, a new model for coding and agent work, priced at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens.

Meanwhile Nvidia, the company whose chips power nearly all of this, said it has stopped shipping its U.S.-licensed Hopper chips to China, after $2.4 billion of such sales last quarter. Interim CEO Colette Kress said the company had no further licensed Hopper shipments planned. That comes days after Nvidia reported earnings in its first quarter without founder Jensen Huang, who resigned November 12.

What does any of this mean for regular folks? A few plain things. First, AI is no longer a startup story — it is a public-market story, with trillion-dollar valuations and the kind of scrutiny that comes with them. Second, the companies building these tools are now competing in the open, and the quality of the models matters more than the marketing, because customers and investors can both see the results. Third, if you use AI tools in your business — and plenty of contractors do now, for estimates, emails, and paperwork — the tools themselves are about to change fast, because the companies behind them are under real pressure to prove they are worth what the market says they are.

No predictions here, and no investment advice. Just the facts: the biggest IPO in American history starts trading today, and the company it values is in a knife fight with the biggest names in tech. That is worth watching, whatever line of work you are in.
