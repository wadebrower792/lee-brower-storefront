# OpenAI Made $10 Billion Last Quarter — and Lost $10.9 Billion Doing It

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 11, 2026

OpenAI shared its latest results with investors on Tuesday, November 10, and the Wall Street Journal reported the numbers the same day. Revenue hit $10 billion in the third quarter — a jump of roughly 50% from the second quarter. For a company that size, that kind of growth in three months is remarkable.

The adjusted operating loss for the same quarter was $10.9 billion. Read that again: OpenAI lost more money than it brought in.

## The numbers

Revenue of $10 billion, up about 50% quarter over quarter. The quarter before that, growth was only 18% — so the third quarter was an acceleration. A year ago, in the third quarter of 2025, OpenAI reported $3.9 billion in sales. The company has nearly tripled its revenue in a year.

But the losses grew too. The adjusted operating loss ticked up from $10.1 billion in the second quarter to $10.9 billion in the third. The adjusted operating margin improved from minus 150% to minus 109% — better, but still deeply negative. For every dollar OpenAI brought in, it spent more than two. Stock-based compensation added another $2.6 billion on top, and the loss figures exclude it.

The Journal's figures come from documents OpenAI shared with investors, viewed by the Journal. OpenAI is a private company, so these are not public filings — they are the numbers the company itself is showing the people who fund it.

## The comparison that stings

The timing matters. OpenAI shared these results just as Anthropic — the maker of Claude — is preparing its much-anticipated IPO next week. Anthropic recently disclosed $17 billion in revenue for the same three-month period, with an operating loss of $567 million.

Put the two side by side: Anthropic brought in $7 billion more and lost about $10.3 billion less. OpenAI is still the bigger name in the public's mind. By these numbers, it is no longer the bigger business.

## What it means for the rest of us

Nobody reading this blog is an OpenAI investor, and this is not investment advice. But there is a practical lesson here for any small business owner using AI tools every day.

The AI tools you use — the chatbots, the image generators, the writing helpers — are cheap to use right now because the companies behind them are spending billions more than they make to win the market. OpenAI spent $10.9 billion in a quarter to earn $10 billion. That is not a sustainable price for the service. It is a land grab, paid for by investors.

So use the tools while they are cheap. Learn them, build with them, let them save you hours. But do not build your whole business on the assumption that today's price is forever. Sooner or later, the companies burning billions have to charge what the service actually costs — or find someone else to pay for it. The smart move is to get the value out of AI now, and keep your business standing on work and customers that are yours, not on a subsidy that can disappear.
