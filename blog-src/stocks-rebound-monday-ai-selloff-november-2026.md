# Wall Street Bounced Back Monday — After the AI Sell-Off Put Every Major Index in Correction

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 23, 2026

After one of the roughest weeks Wall Street has seen in years, stocks bounced hard on Monday morning. Futures pointed sharply higher before the open, with investors stepping back into the same AI-linked shares that got hammered last week. Whether it's a real turn or just a breather is the question nobody can answer yet — but the numbers from last week are worth understanding plainly.

Start with what happened Friday. The S&P 500 closed at 6,910.39, down 8.6 percent for the week, and that put it more than 10 percent below its recent record high — the definition of a correction. The Dow finished at 48,147.33, down about 8 percent on the week, also in correction. The Nasdaq Composite closed at 22,617.73 after a roughly 10 percent weekly drop — its worst week since March 2020 — and it is now down for the year. The Nasdaq-100 was down 18 percent from its peak, just short of bear-market territory, and the S&P 500 had its worst week since April 2025 while the Dow had its worst in more than six years.

The selling was driven by growing doubt about the AI boom — whether the huge spending on AI infrastructure will pay off, and whether demand holds up. The pressure built all week and intensified after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang abruptly departed, and after Anthropic's highly anticipated stock market debut on Thursday failed to lift confidence. Anthropic shares fell 8.5 percent on Friday alone.

Then came Monday. At 7:01 a.m. Eastern, Dow futures were up 971 points, or 2.01 percent. S&P 500 futures were up 192 points, or 2.77 percent. Nasdaq-100 futures were up 862.75 points, or 3.43 percent. Chip stocks led the rebound: Nvidia rose 8 percent in premarket trading, rebounding from its longest weekly losing streak since 2008, while AMD gained 4.5 percent, Broadcom 5.4 percent, and Micron 5.3 percent. The AI infrastructure names that took the worst of last week's beating bounced too — CoreWeave up 7.4 percent, IREN up 8 percent, Nebius up 9.4 percent.

There was real news behind some of the moves. Nvidia announced it reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO and president, effective immediately. And CrowdStrike said it would buy cybersecurity provider Zscaler in an all-stock deal valuing Zscaler at about $43.9 billion — Zscaler jumped 16 percent on the news while CrowdStrike fell 8.2 percent.

Now, a word of plain caution. Some market watchers aren't buying the bounce. One strategist told CNN the sell-off "got a bit overdone" and called Monday's move a "dead cat bounce" — trader talk for a brief recovery before a downtrend resumes. Another pointed out that the S&P 500 is down more than 10 percent from its October 6 high, and that "it's very unusual to see a 10% drawdown so quickly from an all-time high... another substantial selloff is quite likely on that basis." Deutsche Bank analysts wrote Monday that what began in October as a rotation out of tech "has morphed into a broader risk-off move amid a succession of negative catalysts."

I'm not here to tell you what to buy or sell — that's not what this blog does, and it's not my lane. What I will say, as a man who builds things for a living, is this: a market that drops 10 percent in six weeks and then jumps 3 percent before breakfast is a market that doesn't know what it believes yet. The AI buildout is real construction — data centers, power, fiber, concrete — and that work will go on regardless of what the ticker does on a Monday. But the prices on the screen and the value of the work are two different things, and last week was a reminder of how fast the screen can change its mind.

Markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving and close early at 1 p.m. Eastern on Friday, so this is a short week. The S&P Global November Composite PMI reading comes out later Monday, and that will be the next real data point. Watch what happens after the holiday, not just this morning.
