# America May Lose Its Measles-Free Status After 26 Years. Here's What That Means.

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 13, 2026

The United States has held measles elimination status since 2000 — 26 years. That status is now under review, and the experts watching it say losing it is a near-certainty. The final decision is expected November 17.

First, what the numbers say. The CDC's November 6 update reported 5,182 confirmed measles cases in the United States this year through November 5. That's more than double the 2,289 cases recorded in all of 2025, and the most in a single year since 1991 — before elimination was declared. The CDC's update also counted 43 new outbreaks in 2026, with 96% of confirmed cases tied to an outbreak. At least eight people in the United States have died from measles this year.

No state has been hit harder than Pennsylvania, where state health officials documented 1,398 confirmed cases as of October 28, along with 265 hospitalizations and five measles-associated deaths.

Now, what "elimination status" actually means — because the phrase gets misunderstood. Elimination does not mean zero cases. It means the virus is not circulating continuously inside the country: any case that comes in from abroad gets stopped before the same strain keeps spreading for 12 months or more. The Pan American Health Organization, the World Health Organization's regional office for the Americas, is the body that decides. Its Regional Verification Commission is meeting in Washington from November 10 through November 13 to review the U.S. case counts, transmission chains, and lab evidence. Genetic analysis of recent samples indicates the virus has now been circulating continuously in the United States, like an endemic disease.

Why did it get here? One big piece is vaccination coverage. CDC data show measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR) vaccination among U.S. kindergarteners fell to 92.4% in the 2025–2026 school year, down from 95.2% in 2019–2020. Measles is one of the most contagious diseases there is, and it needs very high coverage to stay stopped. When coverage slips a few points, the virus finds the gaps.

To be clear about what is and isn't decided: no final ruling has been announced yet. The commission's decision is expected November 17. If the status is withdrawn, measles would be reclassified from an imported disease to an endemic one — meaning it circulates continuously within the U.S. population. Canada already lost its elimination status at the same commission's November 2025 meeting.

CDC Director Dr. Erica Schwartz told Reuters on November 10 that the agency would keep working with state health departments and promoting vaccination regardless of the outcome. The response doesn't change either way.

For a working family, the takeaway is practical, not political. Measles is preventable with a vaccine that has been around for decades. If you have kids or grandkids and you're not sure their shots are current, your doctor or your county health department can check the record in a few minutes. A disease we beat 26 years ago is back because we let our guard down. The fix is the same one that worked the first time.
