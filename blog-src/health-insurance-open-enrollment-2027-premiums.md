# Health Insurance Open Enrollment Opens Today — and Benchmark Premiums Are Up 13 Percent

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 1, 2026

Open enrollment for 2027 health coverage on HealthCare.gov opens today, Sunday, November 1, 2026. It runs through January 15, 2027. And it opens with a price jump that every household shopping on the marketplace needs to see before picking a plan.

Benchmark Silver plan premiums will cost 13 percent more on average in 2027, according to a KFF analysis published October 30, 2026, based on newly released federal plan data. That follows a 26 percent jump from 2025 to 2026. Put the two years together, KFF says, and the average benchmark premium is up 42 percent between 2025 and 2027.

## What the 13 percent figure actually is

The benchmark Silver plan is the second-lowest-cost Silver plan in your area, and federal subsidies are pegged to it. When KFF says benchmark premiums rise 13 percent on average, it means that reference price — not your personal premium. Your own price depends on where you live, your age, your income, and the plan you pick. The increase is not even: 11 percent in states that use HealthCare.gov, 16 percent in states that run their own marketplaces.

## The dates that decide everything

On HealthCare.gov, open enrollment runs November 1, 2026, through January 15, 2027. Enroll by December 15, 2026, for coverage starting January 1, 2027. Enroll from December 16 through January 15, and your coverage generally starts February 1, 2027 instead. State-run marketplaces may have different dates, so check your own state's site if you do not use HealthCare.gov.

One date confusion is now settled: a 2025 federal rule would have ended HealthCare.gov enrollment on December 15, 2026. A federal court vacated that rule in June 2026, and the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) confirms the window runs through January 15, 2027.

## Why prices look different this year

This is the second year of marketplace coverage without the enhanced federal premium help that expired at the end of 2025. Under the original Affordable Care Act rules that apply again for 2027, there is a hard cutoff: earn more than the limit for your household size, and your premium tax credit drops to zero. Households above that line pay the full, higher 2027 premium with no credit at all.

CMS, in its own Plan Year 2027 fact sheet released October 30, did not give an average premium change. It focused on what is still affordable: more than 75 percent of Americans who buy coverage through HealthCare.gov will continue to have access to premiums at or below $50 a month after tax credits. CMS also said shoppers have access to about 100 different plans on average from five to six insurance companies.

Both things can be true at once. The benchmark price is up sharply, and most subsidized shoppers still see a low monthly premium after credits — while households above the subsidy cutoff face the full increase with no help.

## Two more numbers to know before you shop

First, the out-of-pocket maximum — the most you pay in a year before the plan pays 100 percent — rises to $12,000 for an individual and $24,000 for a family in 2027, up from $10,600 and $21,200, per CMS guidance.

Second, the market shrank last year. Plan selections fell by 1.2 million during the 2026 open enrollment period, and paid-up marketplace enrollment was down 12 percent, or 2.6 million people, to 19.2 million by February 2026, according to KFF.

## The practical move

Do not renew on autopilot. The plan that was cheapest for you last year may not be cheapest this year, and the benchmark change moves the subsidy math for everyone. Compare the full cost — premium plus deductible plus out-of-pocket maximum — not just the monthly number. And if you want coverage starting January 1, the December 15 deadline is the one that matters.
