# Eleven Days Later, Jensen Huang Is Nvidia's CEO Again

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 24, 2026

Eleven days after he resigned, Jensen Huang is back running Nvidia. The company announced Monday, November 23, that it was reinstating Huang as chief executive, effective immediately — one of the fastest reversals in the history of a company this size.

Huang resigned on November 12 after reports of extramarital affairs became public, including one with a businesswoman U.S. authorities suspect of spying for China, an allegation Beijing denies. The board opened an investigation, and the man who co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and built it into the company at the center of the artificial-intelligence boom was gone within days. Now he's back — after the board said it had gained confidence that he does not pose a national security risk, and after President Donald Trump publicly called for his return.

The market's reaction was about as loud as it gets. Nvidia shares leapt 33% in early trading Monday. The Nasdaq rallied 6% and the S&P 500 rose 4%, making up for Friday's plunge. Reuters called it Nvidia's biggest one-day jump in 23 years. Short sellers who had bet against the stock were staring at about $12.2 billion in paper losses on Monday, their biggest one-day hit since at least 2019, according to data firm ORTEX. The company added roughly $950 billion in market value overnight.

But step back from the fireworks and the picture is more complicated. Even after that record jump, Nvidia is still down 32% from its early-October peaks, and about 5% below where it stood before Huang stepped down on November 12. The stock had nearly halved in the six weeks before Monday. One day of euphoria doesn't undo six weeks of selling.

And Huang's return doesn't settle the questions that pushed him out. He is due to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3 at a hearing on U.S. export controls — the same chip-export fight that has shadowed Nvidia's China business for years. Critics in Congress want to know what input senior U.S. officials gave to support his reinstatement, and some are pushing to fast-track export-control legislation. The board chair who said on November 17 that Huang would not be brought back is still there. The investigations into Huang are still running.

For folks in the trades and small business, there's a plain lesson here that has nothing to do with chips. A company can be the most important supplier in the world and still get knocked sideways in a week when leadership becomes the story instead of the work. Nvidia's customers — OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, CoreWeave — buy its chips because the chips work, but the last two weeks showed that trust in the person at the top moves the whole market. Huang built the company, and when he left, the market treated it like the foundation had cracked. When he came back, it treated it like the foundation was poured again.

Whether that trust holds depends on what happens next — the December 3 hearing, the export-control bills, and whether the board and the CEO can actually work together after firing and rehiring the same man in eleven days. The stock says the market believes it. The next few weeks will say whether it was right.
