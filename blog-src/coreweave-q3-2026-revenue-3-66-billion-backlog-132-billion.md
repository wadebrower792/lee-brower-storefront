# CoreWeave Made $3.66 Billion in Three Months — and Still Lost $709 Million

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 11, 2026

CoreWeave reported its third-quarter results on Tuesday evening, November 10, and the numbers tell two stories at once. The AI cloud company — it rents out computing power for artificial intelligence instead of selling it — brought in $3.66 billion in revenue for the quarter. That is up 168% from a year ago, ahead of the $3.54 billion analysts expected, and more than $1 billion higher than the previous quarter alone.

And it still lost $709 million.

## The boom side

Demand is not the problem. CoreWeave's revenue backlog — signed contracts not yet delivered — ended the quarter at a record $132.5 billion, up 138% year over year and up from $104 billion at the end of the second quarter. More than half of that backlog is tied to contracts expected to start delivering by the end of 2026.

The company also raised its full-year guidance, now expecting $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion in revenue for 2026, and it expects to exit the year with annualized run-rate revenue of $20 billion to $21 billion. Adjusted operating income hit $379 million, well above the $200 million to $260 million the company had guided — and the adjusted operating margin moved into double digits, about 10%, a quarter earlier than planned. It was 1% in the first quarter and 5% in the second. The business is getting more efficient as it scales.

Shares rose about 8% in after-hours trading, to $52.58.

## The bill side

Here is the other story. CoreWeave's GAAP operating income was $207 million — real profit from running the business, up from $52 million a year earlier. But interest expense on its debt came to $846 million for the quarter, up from $310 million a year ago. Interest alone was about four times the operating profit. That is how a company with $3.66 billion in sales ends up $709 million in the red, or $1.27 per share.

The debt now stands at $47 billion. Free cash flow for the quarter was negative $7.7 billion — the company spent far more building data centers than it took in. Capital spending in the quarter reached $14.5 billion. CoreWeave expects interest expense to keep rising into the fourth quarter, to between $1.05 billion and $1.15 billion, on revenue of $4.7 billion to $5 billion.

## Why a carpenter should care

CoreWeave does not sell anything you would buy. But it builds data centers — big ones, fast — and it has now doubled its active power footprint to more than 1.7 gigawatts since the start of the year. Every one of those buildings is concrete, steel, electrical, and mechanical work. When a company is spending $14.5 billion in a single quarter on construction and equipment, that is work for somebody's crew, somewhere.

The lesson is plain: the AI boom is real, and it is also expensive. The companies renting out the computing power are growing faster than almost any business in history — and still borrowing heavily to keep up. Growth this fast, funded this way, does not last forever in this shape. But while it lasts, the building keeps going.
