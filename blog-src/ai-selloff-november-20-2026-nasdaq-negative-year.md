# The AI Stock Rout Hit a New Low Friday — the Nasdaq Is Now Down for the Year

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 21, 2026

Friday was the worst day yet in the sell-off that has been eating the stock market for weeks. The S&P 500 fell 3.7 percent, dropping 265.44 points to close at 6,910.39. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.3 percent, losing 1,261.47 points to close at 22,617.73 — and with that, the Nasdaq wiped out all of its gains for the year. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 949.73 points to 48,147.33.

This was the S&P 500's seventh straight day of losses, its longest losing streak since February 2020, when Wall Street started bracing for the Covid pandemic. It was also the index's worst weekly loss in more than 18 months. For the week, the Dow fell 8 percent, the S&P 500 fell 8.6 percent, and the Nasdaq fell 10 percent.

## The AI trade is unwinding

The selling was centered on the same artificial-intelligence stocks that carried the market up most of this year. Chipmakers took the worst of it: Advanced Micro Devices fell 13.3 percent, Micron Technology fell 13.9 percent, and Broadcom fell 12.1 percent. Nvidia, the chipmaker at the center of the whole AI trade, fell 14 percent on Friday — its worst day in almost two years. Nvidia has now fallen 47.5 percent since its October 6 peak, shedding $2.7 trillion in market value.

The Nasdaq hit a record high on October 6 and was up as much as 18.7 percent for the year. Since that peak, the index has shed roughly $8.7 trillion in market value. Both the S&P 500 and the Dow are now in correction territory, meaning each is down more than 10 percent from its recent high.

Even the week's big success story could not stop the slide. Anthropic, the AI lab, went public Thursday in what was reported as the biggest IPO on record. Its shares fell Friday anyway, closing at $88.91 — still above the $83 IPO price, but down from the debut. The sell-off spread around the globe, too: Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's Kospi each fell more than 5 percent overnight.

## Why investors are worried

The worry, in plain terms, is that AI stocks got too expensive and companies are spending too much money building data centers. Investors are asking whether the huge spending is justified by the money coming back. That question has been hanging over the market for weeks, and Friday it turned into a rout.

There are other pressures in the mix. Investors are also watching new state leaders who may slow data-center construction, and uncertainty around Nvidia's future after CEO Jensen Huang resigned. None of that is settled yet, which is exactly why the market is jumpy.

## What it means for regular folks

If you have a 401(k) or an IRA, you probably saw the balance dip again on Friday. Here is the honest part: this is news, not advice, and one bad week is not a plan. Retirement money is meant to ride out years, not days, and the steady advice in a week like this is to be cautious about making sudden changes in response to market swings.

What is certain is that the AI trade, which carried the market for most of the year, is no longer a one-way ride. Six weeks ago the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq were at record highs. Today the Nasdaq is in the red for the year. That is how fast a market can turn when the story changes.
