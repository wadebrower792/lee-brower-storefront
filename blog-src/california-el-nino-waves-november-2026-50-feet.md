# California's Biggest Waves in a Decade — 50-Foot Breakers, and More Swell Coming

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 18, 2026

The biggest waves in more than a decade slammed the California coast on Saturday, November 14, and they're not done yet. The National Weather Service issued a High Surf Warning from the North Bay to Monterey Bay and Big Sur, calling for breaking waves of 25 to 35 feet, with possible 50-foot sets at Mavericks near Half Moon Bay.

The measurements back it up. A buoy offshore Point Reyes measured significant wave heights of 31 feet — the second largest in three decades of records there, topped only during the December 2015 El Niño winter. Waves taller than 50 feet were recorded in San Mateo County on Saturday, and at Pacifica Pier waves reached between 30 and 50 feet. The surf flooded streets, crashed over seawalls, and sent crowds to the coast to watch — while the weather service warned everyone to keep a safe distance.

What makes this round different is what's underneath it. A strong El Niño is warming the eastern Pacific and pushing tides more than a foot higher than a typical year. NOAA measured this El Niño at 2.0 degrees Celsius above normal earlier in November, close behind the very strong 1997–98 event at the same point in the season. El Niño doesn't make the waves by itself — a North Pacific storm does that — but it shifts storm tracks closer to California and lifts sea level, so the waves hit harder. During strong El Niños, wave power along the California coast runs 32% stronger than a typical year.

And the ocean isn't settling down. A storm system arrived in Northern California on the evening of Tuesday, November 17, moving south across the state through Thursday into Friday. Forecasters expect breaking waves of 13 to 17 feet along the Northern and Central California coasts, with occasional sets reaching up to 25 feet, and up to 20-foot breaking waves on the Central coast Thursday, November 19. More strong swells are expected to peak Wednesday and Thursday, with a larger round likely Thursday and Friday, November 19 and 20. NOAA also expects higher-than-normal tides from November 22 to 27 — right through Thanksgiving week.

The safety message from the National Weather Service is plain: stay out of the water and stay off jetties, piers, and rocks. "Dangerously large breaking waves can overpower swimmers, pulling people into the sea from beaches, jetties, and rocks," the weather service warned. Seven or eight more pulses of energy are possible through Thanksgiving week, according to NWS meteorologist Joe Merchant, so the ocean will stay unsettled for a while.

If you're anywhere near the California coast this week, watch from high ground and well back from the water's edge. A wave that big doesn't give you a second chance.
