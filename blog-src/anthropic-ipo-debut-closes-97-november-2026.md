# Anthropic's First Day on the Stock Market: Up 17%, the Biggest IPO Ever

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 20, 2026

The biggest stock market debut in history happened yesterday, and it belonged to an artificial intelligence company. Anthropic, the San Francisco company behind the Claude AI assistant, started trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday, November 19, under the ticker symbol ANT — and finished its first day up 17 percent.

Here is what the numbers say. Anthropic priced its shares at $83 each, above its original target range of $70 to $78. When trading opened, the stock jumped to $111.50, a 34 percent pop. It gave back some of that during the day and closed at $97.17, up $14.17 — 17.07 percent — from the IPO price. About 840 million shares changed hands on the first day, worth roughly $80 billion.

The offering itself set a record. Anthropic sold 980 million shares and raised about $81.3 billion, passing SpaceX's $75 billion listing from June 2026 to become the largest initial public offering ever. At the $83 IPO price, the company was valued at about $1.5 trillion; measured at the closing price, its fully diluted value came to about $1.76 trillion. The offering was reportedly six times oversubscribed — meaning investors wanted about six times more shares than were available.

## Why this debut mattered

This was not a calm week for the AI trade. The same day Anthropic went public, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell more than 1,000 points in a broad selloff, and AI stocks have been under pressure for weeks over worries about an AI bubble, heavy data-center spending, and tighter regulation from the incoming Democratic-led Congress. Anthropic's debut also came after the stunning ouster of Nvidia's chief executive in a possible Chinese spying scandal, which rattled the whole sector.

Against that backdrop, a smooth, record-sized debut was a real test — and it passed. CEO Dario Amodei rang the Nasdaq opening bell on Thursday. Barron's noted that the average first-day return for U.S. IPOs between 1980 and 2025 was 19 percent, according to University of Florida professor Jay Ritter — so Anthropic's 17 percent gain sits right in line with a normal hot IPO, not a wild blowout.

The business behind the ticker is large and growing fast. Anthropic's revenue reached $17 billion in the third quarter of 2026, roughly twelve times what it was a year earlier, according to the company's filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission. The company says it will use the money raised to pay for data centers, chips, and other infrastructure it needs to train its AI models.

## What comes next

The hard part starts now. Like most big IPOs, Anthropic has lock-up periods: insiders and early shareholders generally cannot sell for 180 days, and when that lock-up expires, about 3.7 billion shares become eligible to sell. Only about 8.6 percent of the company's shares are public today, so most of the stock has not hit the market yet.

None of this is investment advice — it is the news, plainly stated. But for anyone who uses AI tools to get work done, the debut is a milestone worth knowing about: the company that builds one of the most widely used AI assistants in the world is now a public company, valued at more than $1.7 trillion on day one, and the whole market was watching to see whether the AI boom still had room to run. On day one, at least, the answer was yes.
