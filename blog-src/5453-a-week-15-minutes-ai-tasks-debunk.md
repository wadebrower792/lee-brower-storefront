# $5,453 a Week for 15 Minutes a Day? Show Me the Proof.

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 10, 2026

Here's an ad that's still running: "Earn up to $5,453 a week with just 15 minutes a day of simple AI tasks. No experience needed."

Stop and do the math with me. Fifteen minutes a day is an hour and forty-five minutes a week. Five thousand four hundred and fifty-three dollars a week for less than two hours of work. That's over $21,000 a month. That's more than most people make in three months, for work you could do before breakfast.

If that were real, nobody would have a job. Everybody would be doing fifteen minutes of AI tasks and collecting five grand a week. The whole country would quit on Monday.

It isn't real. A check of this claim published in November 2026 found no public evidence behind the number at all. No pay stubs. No platform showing that rate. No typical earnings. Just the ad, and a fee to get in the door.

Here's what the law says about it. The Federal Trade Commission has a formal Notice of Penalty Offenses Concerning Money-Making Opportunities. It says false money-making claims are unfair and deceptive and violate the FTC Act. The FTC has put businesses on notice that false money-making claims can lead to big penalties. The rule is simple: if you sell people a way to make money, you have to back up your income claims with evidence. The burden is on the seller, not on you.

And here's what legitimate AI task work actually looks like. Real platforms have an application or a qualification step. They show you the rate before you start. They don't charge you a fee to join. The pay is real, and it's modest — task work pays by the task, and nobody is paying five grand a week for fifteen minutes a day.

So what does this mean for a working person? It means the words "up to" are doing all the heavy lifting in that ad. "Up to $5,453 a week" can mean one person, one time, in circumstances you'll never see — or nobody at all. And "15 minutes a day" is the hook that makes it feel free. The truth is boring and nobody puts it in an ad: real online income takes real work, real clients, and real time, and the honest numbers are hundreds a month at the start, not thousands a week.

If someone can't show you real, typical earnings — not a screenshot, not a testimonial, real numbers — walk away and keep your money.
