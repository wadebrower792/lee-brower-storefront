# ChatGPT Just Opened a Storefront — and Small Sellers Can Claim a Spot

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, November 12, 2026

On Tuesday, November 10, OpenAI launched something called Businesses in ChatGPT, in beta. In plain English: ChatGPT is no longer just a place people ask questions. It's becoming a place where businesses can be found, verified, and talked to — right inside the chat.

Here's how it works. OpenAI built a dashboard called Business Hub. If you own a business, you can claim your profile, verify it, and fix the details ChatGPT shows about you — your website, your description, your logo, your delivery and return policies. You can add a product feed with your prices and what's in stock. And you can see aggregated numbers on how often ChatGPT mentions your business and how many people click through to your site, over a rolling 28 days.

The second piece is called Business Agents. An eligible business can build its own chatbot that lives inside ChatGPT and answers shoppers' questions about its products, using information the business itself supplies. For now, Business Agents are only for ecommerce and online-service businesses in the United States. A business can also sponsor its agent as a destination for ChatGPT ads.

Now, the honest part. Claiming a profile does not guarantee ChatGPT will rank you higher or send you more sales — OpenAI says so itself. And a Business Agent cannot complete a purchase, a booking, or an order check. It answers questions. The customer still has to go to your store to buy. OpenAI is building the storefront first and the checkout later — if it ever builds the checkout at all.

So what does this mean for a small seller like me? A few things. First, the audience is real. OpenAI says 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT every week. That's OpenAI's own number — no independent source has confirmed it — but even if it's half that, it's an audience no single website can match. Second, it's free to claim a profile. The announcement mentions no cost for claiming one. Third, Shopify merchants get a shortcut: eligible products already reach ChatGPT through the Shopify Catalog, and you can connect your store straight from the Shopify dashboard.

The catch is the same one it always is with a platform that isn't yours. OpenAI decides who qualifies, which categories get agents, and what the rules are. It can refuse or delay a whole sector even after you've verified. And the most visible space — the sponsored agent spots — is sold separately. You're building on somebody else's ground, and they hold the keys.

Still, for a small business, a free verified listing in front of that many people is worth claiming. It costs nothing, it corrects wrong information about your business that ChatGPT may already be showing, and it gives you a simple scoreboard: how often the chatbot talks about you, and how many people click through.

The rule I keep coming back to: own your store, use their platform. My storefront and my Gumroad store are mine. A ChatGPT listing is a signpost pointing at them — not a replacement for them. Claim the free spot, keep your own house in order, and let the chatbot send people home to you.
