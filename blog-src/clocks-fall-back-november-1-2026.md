# Clocks Fall Back Sunday, November 1: What Actually Changes

By Wade Lee Brower Jr, October 30, 2026

Daylight saving time ends at 2 a.m. local time on Sunday, November 1, 2026. At that moment, clocks move back one hour to 1 a.m., and we return to standard time. If you have a clock that does not update itself, set it back before bed on Saturday night, October 31, and you will be set for Sunday morning.

## Why this year is the earliest it can be

Under current federal law, daylight saving time begins on the second Sunday in March and ends on the first Sunday in November. Because November 1 falls on a Sunday in 2026, this year's change lands on the earliest date the law allows — the window runs November 1 through November 7 depending on how the calendar falls. That is also why Halloween and the time change land back to back this year: trick-or-treating happens Saturday night, and the clocks fall back in the early hours of Sunday morning.

The practical effect is simple and often misunderstood. We gain an hour by the clock — the hour from 1 a.m. to 2 a.m. happens twice — but the change does not create more daylight. It shifts an hour of daylight from the evening to the morning. Sunrises and sunsets both happen about an hour earlier by the clock than they did the day before. The National Institute of Standards and Technology describes it the same way: an hour of daylight moves from evening to morning.

## Who does not change

Most of the country turns the clocks back, but not all of it. Hawaii and most of Arizona do not observe daylight saving time, so they do not fall back. If you have family, crews, or clients in those places, your call times with them shift by an hour after Sunday even though nothing changed on their end. The same goes for anyone coordinating across time zones: double-check Sunday appointments and Monday morning start times rather than assuming everyone moved together.

## The bill that did not change anything — yet

You may have heard that Congress was going to end the twice-yearly clock change. The Sunshine Protection Act, which would make daylight saving time permanent year-round, passed the House in July 2026 by a bipartisan vote reported as 308-117. But it has not cleared the Senate, and no change has been enacted into law. Reporting as of late October 2026 says the Senate has not voted on it and is in an extended recess around the November 3 election. Until both chambers pass it and the president signs it, the current system stands — and that means we fall back on November 1.

Unless Congress changes the law, daylight saving time returns on Sunday, March 14, 2027, when clocks spring forward one hour. The next fall-back after this one would be Sunday, November 7, 2027.

## A working person's checklist

For a contractor, the time change is small but it bites if you ignore it. Confirm Monday start times with every crew and subcontractor, because one person on the old time is a crew standing around. Check any timers, jobsite lights, and security cameras that run on a clock rather than a network — those are the ones that will be an hour off on Monday. And if you schedule work by daylight, remember the evening light is gone an hour earlier now: plan the outdoor finish work for the morning side of the day.

One hour, one Sunday, twice a year. Set the clocks Saturday night and get on with the week.
